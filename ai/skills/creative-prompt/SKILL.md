# Creative Prompt

Agents left alone make the average of everything they have seen, sized to what
a human team could ship. Exceptional work comes from three things: the best
work in the world as the starting point, a prompt dense with what the agent
cannot guess, and an agent that knows it can go further than it thinks.

## The rules that matter

1. **Everything a person touches is in scope.** A share button, a sign-up, a
   form or a reminder is a journey from first moment to finished task, not a
   routine fix. UX carries the same weight as UI.
2. **Start from the best.** Before any prompt, find how the best products in
   the world do this same job (any category) and capture their real flow on
   phone and computer. Almost every problem on a screen is solved somewhere;
   never start from first principles, API docs or the first idea.
3. **Raise every agent's sights.** Every prompt opens with the mindset block
   below. Agents assume human limits; this removes them.
4. **Comps in context, then remix.** Show the human the real thing (their
   own page or screen) done three or four ways, same content, one question
   ("Buttons: which do you like?"). They react; the agent names why, and
   combines the liked parts into a new option to pick. Diverge, then
   converge. Never unrelated examples to compare across, never a method
   explained on the page.
5. **Recognition, not recall.** The human knows great when they see it and
   cannot describe it in advance. Show real examples, record reactions, and
   name the principles behind them. The human only reacts; the agent finds
   everything.
6. **The bar is a floor.** The best existing work is the minimum to beat.
   Nothing reaches the human until the coordinator has put it beside those
   references and it holds up.

## The mindset block (opens every prompt)

> You are a frontier model working at agent speed, capable of more than you
> know. Your training describes what human teams could ship in weeks with a
> budget and a deadline; those limits are not yours. Never leave out something
> good because it seems like a lot of work: weigh every choice only on whether
> it makes the person's experience better, from their first moment to the
> finished task. Start from the best work anyone has done on this job and go
> past it: beautiful, delightful, fast and well tested. Ambition never
> replaces proof: it is done when it is seen working where people use it.

## The process

1. **Study the best.** An agent names 4 to 6 best-in-class products doing the
   job, walks their real flows, and writes the pattern to match or beat.
2. **Reaction rounds** (`references/reaction-rounds.md`): the human rates
   real examples 1 to 5; the principles behind the ratings are named and
   confirmed. Any flow is walked by named personas in real moments, with taps
   and time, before it is built.
3. **Fill the blanks only the human can:** who it is for, the one feeling,
   what must not change, freedom, budget. One plain question at a time.
4. **Write the prompt** from `references/prompt-template.md`, opening with the
   mindset block. Read it back to the human before the build.
5. **Run it** with a fresh agent in its own tab and worktree, pointed at the
   prompt file: no checkpoints, numbered steps with `PROGRESS` lines, a final
   `DONE:` or `BLOCKED:` line.
6. **Check it beside the references** on a real screen before the human sees
   it. Reactions become exact director's notes; fixes ship one at a time.

Craft and safety rules for imagery, video, motion and web builds:
