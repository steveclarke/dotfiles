# Copywriting: how a specialist does it

Words people read (bios, about pages, invitations, headlines, emails) are written the way an agency copywriter writes them, not lifted from notes.

## Gather first: the intake

A copywriter never writes a bio from a list of duties. Before drafting, they interview the person, or someone who knows them, with a short questionnaire. For a person's profile:

1. What do people come to you for? (the role in their own words, not the title)
2. What do you love about this place or this work?
3. What would surprise people about you?
4. One small thing you love: a habit, a place, a pastime.
5. How would a friend describe you in three words?
6. Anything you would rather not be on a public page?

Ask the person themselves where possible; a second-hand list ("she sings, she cleans the kitchen") gives facts, not character. Send it as one short form with a deadline. When answers are thin, ask a follow-up rather than guess.

## Write: character, then one telling detail

- Lead with who the person is to the people reading (what you would go to them for), in plain warm words.
- Add one telling detail: a single small, true, specific thing that makes the person real. One, not a list.
- Active voice that gives people credit for what they do ("Janice sets the family feel of this church", never "she loves the family feel").
- Leave out anything that dates quickly (how someone currently teaches), anything a reader could take the wrong way (old friendships between leaders), and duties nobody outside cares about.
- Two or three sentences per person; leaders first and fuller, the rest shorter and even.
- Draft two or three options for any line that sets the tone (a section intro, a headline) and let the human pick.

## Check

Read each line aloud as a newcomer would. Cut any word that sounds clever, contrived or like a résumé. If a line could describe anyone, it needs the telling detail.
